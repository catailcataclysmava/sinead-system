PARAGON additions

The chart began as a threat-response tool, so it sorted powers by how dangerous they are and how they're delivered. Support powers used to be filed under whatever fit their delivery (a touch healer became a Striker). PARAGON added Mender and Enabler so support capes are rated for what they actually do.

  • Mender vs Enabler: a Mender fixes what's wrong (injuries, illness, exhaustion). An Enabler makes people better than their baseline.
  • Enabler vs Trump: an Enabler boosts people. A Trump acts on powers themselves: granting, removing, copying or altering them.

Ratings

Every classification carries a rating from 1 to 10 for how effective that aspect of the power is, e.g. Brute 7 or Thinker 2 / Trump 1. Conditional powers can be given as a range (Brute 2–6).

RatingMeaningTypical response
1–2Minor. Barely beyond a trained human.Standard procedure
3–4Notable. Useful in a fight, dangerous if misused.Engage with care
5–6Serious. Can change the outcome of an incident on its own.Team response
7–8Major. Regional heavy hitter.Class-A involvement
9–10Extreme. City-level or worse.Multiple Class-A teams; evacuation
  • Hero classes and ratings are separate: a hero's Class (A, B, C) ranks the hero or team; the rating describes the power. Class-C capes usually top out at 2–4, Class-B at 4–6 and Class-A at 6–8.
